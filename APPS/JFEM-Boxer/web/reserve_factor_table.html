Only RF < 1 filters the table and its exports to fully assessed values strictly below 1. Unavailable results and RF = 1 are excluded. Edit property opens a local property editor for generated models. Dimensions are shown in mm; the shared minimum gauges remain unchanged. A value below its minimum turns both the input and originating property cell red and must be corrected before applying. Applied edits update the Study. The table retains the previous assessment with a model/results mismatch notice, so you can continue editing several properties. Its model properties, allowables and fixities stay paired with their original results; rerun analysis to update the values. Exports include the same notice. Imported properties are edited in their original input file.

Minimum RF combines the available failure checks while preserving their physical scopes. The critical case and failure mode presentations keep the numerical RF beside the selected label. Read the scope and assessment notes when a whole-property stress check governs a smaller physical region. Yellow means RF is greater than 1 and every relevant dimension is confirmed at its enforced minimum gauge. Unknown, incomplete and partly reducible regions do not receive yellow. A complete RF 99 no-demand sentinel can be yellow at minimum gauge; 99 remains a sentinel, not a computed margin. Other assessed regions retain the selected color scale.

This table follows the load-case selection and envelope method in Results → Stress Report. Change case and envelope controls in the Study window; this tab updates automatically. The failure-mode selector is synchronized with the Study window; displayed results are read-only. For Von Mises stress, each reserve factor is the isotropic material’s allowable divided by the property-region stress envelope. With either red/blue palette, red means below 1, blue means above 1, and 1 is the neutral threshold. Reserve factors are dimensionless. Colors use fixed limits 0 and 2; values above 2 saturate and are written in dark orange. Both red/blue palettes retain a distinct hue immediately below and above 1. Other palette choices match those in the sensitivity tables.

Euler column buckling requires the rib-bay and stringer-section metadata produced by the lifting-surface generator. Imported property IDs alone cannot define a physical column. The assessment uses the recorded physical stringer segment between restraints, its weakest bending rigidity and the user’s end-fixity coefficient c. Critical load = c × π² EI / L², where c = 1/K² (c = 1 for pinned ends and c = 4 for fixed ends). The smallest critical-load / compression ratio across section cuts and selected cases governs. RF 99 means complete recovery with no compression; it is a display sentinel, not a calculated reserve factor. Missing recovery and unsupported section/material definitions remain unassessed. The Euler check does not evaluate local web/flange buckling or nonlinear collapse.

Stringer blade instability assesses the free web of each isotropic T beam stringer between ribs. Its elastic critical stress is σcr = 0.425 × π² E / [12(1 − ν²)] × (t/h)², using the clear web height h (total T height minus attached-flange thickness) and web thickness t. This is the long-plate outstand model: the attached longitudinal edge is simply supported and the opposite edge is free. No rotational restraint or Euler end-fixity credit is applied. If the sections vary, the lowest local critical stress governs. Demand is the equal-weight arithmetic mean of signed axial bar stresses in that rib bay; average the two axial end stresses first when both are recovered. Tension offsets compression. Bending peaks, Von Mises stresses and skin stresses are not included. The smallest critical-stress / average-compression ratio across selected SOL101 and fully converged SOL106 cases governs. RF 99 means that complete recovery gives no average compression; individual bars can still be compressive. Unsupported shell stringers, other section types, missing recovery or stale results remain unassessed. This local elastic plate check does not predict yielding, crippling, flange buckling or nonlinear collapse.

Skin panel buckling uses each actual skin compartment between two ribs and adjacent stringers or spar boundaries. It combines the area-averaged membrane stress components σx, σy and τxy in panel axes: x follows the span and y crosses the panel width. Positive normal stresses are tension, negative values compression; shear retains its sign. A rectangular-plate Ritz eigenvalue calculation uses the actual isotropic or laminate bending stiffness and all three membrane resultants together, including stabilizing tension. The mean lengths of opposite edges define its equivalent length and width. The skin fixity coefficient under Properties → Skin multiplies the plate bending resistance: c = 1 is the simply supported reference. Higher values scale the elastic resistance to represent additional restraint; they do not solve a different set of plate edge conditions. This coefficient is independent of the Euler column coefficient. The smallest converged positive buckling multiplier over the selected load cases is the reserve factor. RF 99 is reserved for complete recovery with no destabilizing averaged membrane state. Local compressive stress can still exist within such a panel. Missing results, unsupported geometry or unconverged plate calculations remain unassessed. This equivalent thin-plate assessment does not model transverse-shear flexibility, sandwich-core instability, face wrinkling, shell curvature, postbuckling, plasticity or global panel interaction.

Spar and rib web buckling uses each recorded physical web compartment and its spatial signed membrane-force field. Stress variations are retained in the plate calculation; the displayed stress components are area averages only. Spar web x follows the spar, rib web x crosses the rib, and y follows web height. The associated fixity controls are under Properties → Spars or Properties → Ribs. Spar cap and rib stiffener column checks use the isolated beam member between recorded restraints, its weakest bending rigidity and recovered compression. They do not credit adjacent skin or web stiffness and do not assess lateral-torsional buckling. Rib-stiffener blade instability applies the same isotropic T free-web equation and signed arithmetic bar-stress averaging as stringer blades. It does not use column end-fixity credit. All checks require complete current results and physical membership metadata. Unsupported definitions remain unassessed. A web-case eigenvalue that has not converged can be certified non-governing only when a conservative lower bound is above an already converged governing case. The case RF remains unresolved; details and exports state its lower bound and the certification explicitly.

For each property, the selected statistic is calculated within each physical SOL101 or fully converged SOL106 load case; the largest case value governs. “Average below percentile” discards only element peaks above that case’s cutoff, retaining ties. Averages weight elements equally. These ratios do not predict nonlinear collapse loads.

Panel and component cells use recorded property ownership. Properties spanning multiple locations are shown once as shared regions; their reserve factor is never presented as a separate local result for each bay. Imported models without lifting-surface ownership use the property list. Missing cases, stale geometry, incomplete recovery, absent allowables and unsupported laminate criteria remain explicitly unassessed. They are not replaced by zero and do not count as assessed regions.

Displayed numbers have at most five significant digits. Stress units are included in column headings and value details. Exports contain one row per property for Von Mises, one per physical stringer region for Euler and blade instability, or one per recorded physical skin/web panel, cap or stiffener region for component buckling, in the current group and filter, with table-view metadata, governing cases and assessment notes. If the Study closes, the last received report remains available and is clearly marked disconnected.
