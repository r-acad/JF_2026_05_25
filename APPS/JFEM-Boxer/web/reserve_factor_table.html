Reserve factors

Connecting to Study…

This table follows the load-case selection and envelope method in Results → Stress Report. Change case and envelope controls in the Study window; this tab updates automatically. The failure-mode selector is synchronized with the Study window; displayed results are read-only. For Von Mises stress, each reserve factor is the isotropic material’s allowable divided by the property-region stress envelope. With either red/blue palette, red means below 1, blue means above 1, and 1 is the neutral threshold. Reserve factors are dimensionless. Colors use fixed limits 0 and 2; values above 2 saturate and are written in dark orange. Both red/blue palettes retain a distinct hue immediately below and above 1. Other palette choices match those in the sensitivity tables.

Euler column buckling requires the rib-bay and stringer-section metadata produced by the lifting-surface generator. Imported property IDs alone cannot define a physical column. The assessment uses the recorded physical stringer segment between restraints, its weakest bending rigidity and the user’s end-fixity coefficient c. Critical load = c × π² EI / L², where c = 1/K² (c = 1 for pinned ends and c = 4 for fixed ends). The smallest critical-load / compression ratio across section cuts and selected cases governs. RF 99 means complete recovery with no compression; it is a display sentinel, not a calculated reserve factor. Missing recovery and unsupported section/material definitions remain unassessed. The Euler check does not evaluate local web/flange buckling or nonlinear collapse.

Stringer blade instability assesses the free web of each isotropic T beam stringer between ribs. Its elastic critical stress is σcr = 0.425 × π² E / [12(1 − ν²)] × (t/h)², using the clear web height h (total T height minus attached-flange thickness) and web thickness t. This is the long-plate outstand model: the attached longitudinal edge is simply supported and the opposite edge is free. No rotational restraint or Euler end-fixity credit is applied. If the sections vary, the lowest local critical stress governs. Demand is the equal-weight arithmetic mean of signed axial bar stresses in that rib bay; average the two axial end stresses first when both are recovered. Tension offsets compression. Bending peaks, Von Mises stresses and skin stresses are not included. The smallest critical-stress / average-compression ratio across selected SOL101 and fully converged SOL106 cases governs. RF 99 means that complete recovery gives no average compression; individual bars can still be compressive. Unsupported shell stringers, other section types, missing recovery or stale results remain unassessed. This local elastic plate check does not predict yielding, crippling, flange buckling or nonlinear collapse.

For each property, the selected statistic is calculated within each physical SOL101 or fully converged SOL106 load case; the largest case value governs. “Average below percentile” discards only element peaks above that case’s cutoff, retaining ties. Averages weight elements equally. These ratios do not predict nonlinear collapse loads.

Panel and component cells use recorded property ownership. Properties spanning multiple locations are shown once as shared regions; their reserve factor is never presented as a separate local result for each bay. Imported models without lifting-surface ownership use the property list. Missing cases, stale geometry, incomplete recovery, absent allowables and unsupported laminate criteria remain explicitly unassessed. They are not replaced by zero and do not count as assessed regions.

Displayed numbers have at most five significant digits. Stress units are included in column headings and value details. Exports contain one row per property for Von Mises, or one per physical stringer region for Euler and blade instability, in the current group and filter, with table-view metadata, governing cases and assessment notes. If the Study closes, the last received report remains available and is clearly marked disconnected.
